Two other kinds of absence. Seen but too thin: 105 locations appear in the sample and clear neither threshold — Gdańsk (7 postings, 3 employers), Katowice (7 postings, 3 employers), Delhi (6 postings, 3 employers) come closest. The employer count is usually what stops them, and one employer hiring is an office, not a centre. Not priceable at all: China (Wuxi, Dalian, Chengdu) and Morocco (Casablanca, Rabat) — ILOSTAT publishes no earnings by occupation for either, so there is no cost figure on the basis every market here uses, and two pillars without the decisive one would be worse than no row. Egypt was dropped for a third reason: its professionals report 1.06× clerical pay against 1.3–2.9× everywhere else, which is a broken series rather than a cheap country.

The boundary of this evidence

What it settles

  • Which cities genuinely advertise this work: 11 clear the evidence threshold, on 109 GBS and GCC postings.
  • That 5 cities finish level at the top. The draws cannot separate them, so their order is not a finding.
  • That the answer turns on cost at your weighting, and how far it moves when you price something else.
  • Who already operates in 11 of them, by name.
  • How far the evidence separates them: 4 bands, not 11 ranks.

What it does not

  • Whether GBS work is actually advertised in Manila, Kuala Lumpur, Lisbon, Bucharest, Prague or Budapest. They are priced above on the five pillars that reach them; the two built from postings do not.
  • What employer charges and attrition actually cost, per market. Exhibit 3 loads the wage with both, but at a rate you set — no free source gives comparable employer-charge schedules or attrition rates for all 11 cities, so the factor is uniform where reality is not.
  • Incentives, property and transition cost. None are in this study. Exhibit 3 loads the wage line and stops there, so it remains one line of a run-cost rather than a business case.
  • Whether a city suits your mandate. Nothing here is a recommendation.

What would change this

Three checks a shortlist validation would run

  1. Live wage and employer-charge quotes for Mumbai, Chennai, Pune, Bangalore and Hyderabad. 5 of the 5 carry ILOSTAT’s national wage, and the 25% employer loading is a uniform assumption where real schedules differ by country. A recruiter’s per-role quote and a payroll provider’s charge schedule replace both — the two figures on Exhibit 3 no public source supplies.
  2. Site visits against the advertised capability. The capability pillar is job postings — as few as 4 behind a city in this band. Postings say who is hiring; they cannot say whether the advertised work is the work done, or whether a centre could hire at programme rate. A provider RFI and days on the ground settle what postings cannot.
  3. Attrition and ramp data from the operators already there. The 42 employers named in this band’s postings already run centres there — the table below lists them per city. Their attrition, time-to-fill and ramp curves would replace the 15% backfill assumption, which is the one Exhibit 3 input that can reorder cities — and today it is a slider.

Checks, not refinements: each replaces an input this analysis cannot source from public data, which is why they close the page rather than extend it. A phase 2 that skips them is trusting a slider.

Method and limitations
  • Scores are relative to the cities on screen, not absolute ratings.
  • Robust means a top-three place in 90% of 2,000 reweightings and at least 10 postings behind it.
  • Setting a pillar's weight to zero shows whether it decides anything. For a transactional hub the top band survives losing any pillar except cost; for a judgment centre five of seven move it.
  • Bands group cities the draws cannot tell apart. A city opens a new band only when every city above it finishes ahead in at least 65% of runs — so cities sharing a band have no established order between them.

9 of the 21 pairs correlate at 0.7 or above, and the average pair sits at 0.58. These 7 pillars therefore carry about 2.0 independent directions between them, so the 2,000 reweightings explore correspondingly less of the decision space than their count suggests.

Why: 5 of the 7 pillars — talent, governance, overlap, durability and employer depth — are national series that take one value per country across these cities. They cannot separate two cities in the same country whatever weight they are given, and the countries themselves line up on close to one axis.

Limits

  • The 7 pillars are not independent: the average pair correlates at 0.58 and they carry about 2.0 independent directions between them, because 5 of the 7 are national series shared by every city in a country. The 2,000 reweightings therefore explore substantially less of the decision space than their count suggests — a top-three place that survives them has survived less than the number sounds like. The matrix is above.
  • One snapshot, 22 August 2026. A city hiring quietly during the fetch is under-represented; absence is weak evidence, not a verdict. Nothing here can show a trend, because there is only one point in time to compare.
  • Only Polish cities have city-level cost. The rest use their country's, so cities within them differ on capability alone — treat that order as undetermined.
  • Capability comes from few postings, as low as five per city. The stability column already accounts for this; the ranking below the top few is not meaningful.
  • The GBS/GCC classifier was audited five times over a hundred postings; two of the last twenty were clearly wrong. That error rate is modelled in the stability column, not just noted.
  • A second job board would not close the coverage gap. Employer depth counts distinct employers within one feed, so another feed’s count is not comparable, and splicing one in would move Manila into the ranking on evidence unlike the rest.
  • Exhibit 3 subtracts a national baseline from cities that sometimes carry a regional index, so a capital-city premium sits on one side of it and not the other. Warsaw against a UK baseline is the clearest case: it reads as dearer than the UK, which is partly Mazowieckie against a British national mean rather than a wage fact.
  • Exhibit 3 is a wage line, not a business case. It excludes facilities, technology, management overhead, transition and severance, and holds headcount one-for-one. Read it as the upper bound on one component of the saving.
  • Six established locations sit outside the ranking because the postings feed does not reach them: Manila, Kuala Lumpur, Bucharest, Prague, Budapest and Lisbon. Five pillars do reach them and are reported under 'Beyond the sample'; capability and employer depth are the two that cannot be, so these markets are never scored against the ranked cities.
